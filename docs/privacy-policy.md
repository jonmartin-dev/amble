# Amble privacy policy

Last updated November 2, 2026

This covers the Amble app for Android. Amble keeps everything you put in it (tasks, lists, notes, links, photos and files) in its own storage on your phone, and nothing leaves the phone unless you turn on or use one of the things below. There's no account to make, no ads and no analytics. Amble has no servers of its own, and its developer never receives your data.

## Sync with Google Drive

If you turn on sync, Amble keeps a copy in your Google Drive and brings your other devices in step with it. For just you the copy is in Amble's own hidden folder in your Drive, which only Amble can open. For a household, it's a file in the Drive of whoever made the household, shared with the people they add. Everyone in it has the whole file, including lists marked as only theirs and how someone said their day was looking, which Amble keeps out of others' view but doesn't keep out of the file.

Amble asks Google only for its own files: the hidden folder, and files it made or that you picked for it. It can't see anything else in your Drive. Android gives the app a key to your Drive that lasts about an hour, and a fresh one while you allow Amble. Photos and files on tasks stay on the phone of whoever added them, unless your household syncs with its own server.

## Your household's own server

If your household runs an Amble server and you sync with it, the server keeps a copy of the household's tasks and lists, and the photos and files added to tasks, so everyone's devices have them. You sign in to it the way it lets you, and it keeps your name and email address and when you last signed in. It's a computer someone in your household keeps, not Amble's.

## Calendars

If you turn on Calendars, Amble reads the calendars on your phone that you choose, through Android's calendar permission, to work out how busy your days are and plan around them. The events from 12 weeks back to 6 months ahead stay on the phone and are read again whenever Amble opens. Only how many minutes of each day are busy goes into the household's copy, so everyone else (and any Amble server) sees that a day is busier but never what's on it. Amble only reads. It never adds or edits or deletes events. Turning Calendars off removes the events from Amble.

## Joining a server by its code

Use my own server, Scan the code opens Google's code scanner, which comes with Google Play services and needs no camera permission. Amble gets only what the code says. When you scan, Google's scanner may send Google diagnostics about it, such as the phone's model and Android version, an install identifier and how the scan went, under [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). Typing the server's address instead skips the scanner.

## Reminders

A reminder you set is handed to your phone, which shows it at its time. With sync on, the phone also checks your household's copy about every 15 minutes for reminders you set elsewhere. Nothing goes through a push service.

## AI features

The AI features are off until you set one up. You can enter your own key for an AI provider such as Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or OpenRouter, and the key stays on your phone. You can also use a model on your own computer. If your household syncs through Google Drive, its admin can set up one model for everyone instead: that key goes into the household's file in Google Drive, where anyone in the household could read it, and each phone calls the provider with it. If your phone syncs with your household's own server, you can use the model its owner set up there, and the key stays on the server.

The text they need goes to that provider when you use them: what you type into quick add, task titles and related details, and with Calendars on, the titles and times of your own events that day or week for the morning's suggestions and planning your week. With your own key or the household's it goes straight from your phone to the provider's API. Through your household's server it goes to the server, which sends it on. Each provider handles it under its own terms.

## Claude and ChatGPT

With a household server, you can connect Claude or ChatGPT to Amble. Then it can read and change your tasks, lists and plans when you ask it to, acting as you, and look at photos and text files the server has. What it reads becomes part of that conversation and is handled under your Anthropic or OpenAI account's privacy settings. You can disconnect it any time on the server's page.

## Permissions

- Internet, for sync and the AI features when you turn them on.
- Calendars, read only, asked for when you turn Calendars on.
- Notifications, and alarms and reminders, to show reminders you set at their time.
- Starting at boot, to set your reminders again after the phone restarts.
- Vibration, for a tick under your finger.

## Sharing

Amble never sells your data or shares it with anyone besides the services above that you set up.

## Removing your data

Stop syncing in Settings, then uninstall Amble or clear its storage in Android's settings to remove everything from your phone. A household file in Google Drive is removed like any other file there. Amble's hidden copy goes when you disconnect Amble at myaccount.google.com/connections. Whoever runs your household's server can remove your account and its copy there.

## Changes and questions

When this policy changes, the new version goes here with a new date. For questions, email the address on Amble's Google Play listing.
